What this is

Everything used to run the workshop, in one place: the decks, the Padlet wall, the run of show, the One-Change Card, and the follow-up. Nothing in the playbook links here. It isn’t secret, just out of the way.

The Idea

Faculty are expected to teach AI literacy while they’re still learning it themselves. The workshop separates the roles on purpose. Faculty first use AI as learners on a real task from their own lives, then look back at that experience as teachers, and finally commit to one change in a course they teach.

The BE behaviors are never taught up front. The five reflection questions seed them, and the reveal names what participants already said.

Run of Show (60 minutes)
Sixty-minute run of show
TimeSegment
0:00–0:05Rule-setting and think-aloud: “Today you are only a learner.” The facilitator does the learner task live for about 3 minutes, narrating what they notice.
0:05–0:25Learner task with coaching. A real task from participants’ own lives, not work.
0:25–0:35Wall reflection: five questions, anonymous posts
—The hinge: “You leave this moment as a learner. You return as a teacher.”
0:35–0:50Teacher-hat replay, cluster by cluster: “What does this tell us about students?”
0:50–0:55The reveal
0:55–1:03One-change commitment on the card
1:03–1:05Close: “AI literacy isn’t something we teach once. It’s something we experience first, and then model.”
The full version: two acts with lunch

The recommended OCTC format, capped at 20–25 people. Lunch is the hinge, and the facilitator curates the wall while everyone eats.

The Five Questions and the Reveal

Each question quietly maps to one behavior, so the wall sorts itself. The columns show only the questions; the behavior names arrive at the reveal.

Five questions mapped to BE behaviors
#Question on the wallRevealed as
1What did you share with the tool — and was there anything you deliberately held back? Why?BE Safe: what you held back
2If you shared this output with someone who mattered, would you mention AI’s role? Would it feel like yours?BE Honest: whether you’d say it was AI
3What happened between receiving the output and deciding what to do with it? Anything?BE Critical: the pause, or the missing one
4Why did you reach for AI for this — was it the right tool, or the nearest tool?BE Responsible: the right tool, or the nearest tool
5Do you understand this better now, or do you just have an answer?BE Reflective: understanding, or an answer
Follow-Up
  • Photograph or collect the One-Change Cards before people leave.
  • About three weeks later, email each person about their own commitment: “How’s your one change going?” Link Exercise 2, where the change gets built.
